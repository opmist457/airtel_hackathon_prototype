1

Purpose Limitation & Minimization (DPDP §5)

Traditional enterprise telecom sales demand paper photocopies of bank statements, utility bills, and personal identity cards. Udyam Digital eliminates data hoarding:

  • Stateless Registry Lookups: Verification connects to national GSTIN (3,534 mock records) and Udyam (2,723 mock records) registries via stateless API lookups. Only the verified status, entity trade name, and validation token are kept; raw query payloads are immediately discarded.
  • No Banking Credential Exposure: Recurring auto-pay mandates use tokenized NPCI / e-NACH handles. Zero bank account numbers or debit cards are stored on platform servers.
  • Static Postal Geometry Only: Feasibility calculation relies strictly on user-supplied premises PIN codes and shop numbers. Persistent GPS or mobile device network tracking is strictly prohibited.
  • Zero Data Monetization: Enterprise records are ring-fenced for connectivity provisioning; cross-selling to 3rd-party advertisers or data brokers is programmatically barred.
2

Granular Consent & Verifiable Audit (DPDP §6)

Per Section 6 of the DPDP Act 2023, consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional, and unambiguous with verifiable proof:

  • Affirmative Pre-KYC Checkpoint: Before uploading documents or triggering automated registry lookup, users encounter an unbundled consent screen detailing purpose, data items collected, and retention boundaries. No hidden terms or pre-ticked boxes.
  • Cryptographic SHA-256 Proof: Each consent submission generates an immutable digital token: {timestamp_utc, principal_id, ip_hash, consent_version, purpose} hashed with SHA-256 for non-repudiation during regulatory audits.
  • Unbundled Channels: Statutory e-KYC consent is completely decoupled from optional promotional communications, WhatsApp alerts, and partner SaaS offers.
  • Unilateral Right to Revoke: Signatories can retract secondary marketing consent anytime via the self-service Order Tracker without interrupting primary telecom services.

Statutory Retention vs. Erasure (DPDP §8(7) & DoT)

While DPDP Act Section 8 requires prompt erasure, Section 8(7) protects sectoral statutory compliance. Airtel reconciles privacy with telecom licensing mandates:

  • Mandatory 5-Year CAF Retention: Under DoT Unified Access License Condition 39.20, customer verification files (CAF, e-KYC records, and authorized signatory tokens) are legally mandated to be retained for exactly 5 years following service disconnection.
  • 2-Year Call Detail Records (CDR): Airtel IQ Cloud PBX and VoIP logs are retained for 2 years for statutory security audits, stored in write-once audit storage.
  • Automated Cryptographic Purge: When the statutory retention period lapses, an automated zero-fill purge engine permanently destroys archived records and issues a deletion receipt.
  • Dropped Lead Sanitization: Incomplete lead applications abandoned during early assessment are permanently wiped from sales caches after 90 days.
